We first study a nurse team of 11 evenly skilled agents over a 10-weeks long frame (70 days). For the sake
of simplicity, we assume the frame begins on a Monday and ends on a Sunday. The service remains open on
the week-end with a different regime.
A day of work is divided in three shifts of 7h30: a morning shift from 6:00 a.m. to 1:30 p.m., an evening
shift from 1:00 p.m. to 8:30 p.m., and a day shift from 10 a.m. to 5:30 p.m.
The service of nurses is under the following constraints:
1. On week days, the service needs 3 morning agents, 2 evening agent and 1 day agent;
2. On week-end days, the service only needs 3 morning agents and 2 evening agents (no day agents are
required);
3. An agent can work at most one of the three daily shifts;
4. There must be at least 12 consecutive hours of rest between two worked shifts: an agent cannot work
in the evening and in the morning the following day;
5. If an agent rests on a week day, their total rest must be over 36 consecutive hours. In other words, an
agent cannot work in the evening, rest the following day and go back to work in the morning the day
after;
6. An agent cannot work more than 5 consecutive days;
7. An agent, supposedly working 35 hours a week in average, cannot exceed 44 hours of work on rolling
weeks, but there is no minimum. They can work 42 hours one week, and 23 hours the following week
for instance;
8. An agent must rest every other week-end and must work every other week-end;
9. There must be 2 rest days for 7 worked days and an agent is granted an additional rest day every
month. For instance, over 4 weeks, there are 2 week-ends of full rest (4 days), so 4 more rest days must
be attributed over the week days on this period. Moreover, an extra rest day is granted, which makes
a total of 9 rest days for 4 weeks of work, with 4 on week-end days among them;
10. 3 of the agents have a part-time contract and work 80% of the time: this means they must have an
extra rest day which cannot be taken on week-ends;
11. There cannot be a worked day between two rest days;
12. The schedule frame must be cyclical: the schedule of the second agent has to be the same as the first
agent with a gap of 7 days, etc.;
13. Extra-staff must be avoided on week-ends;
14. There must be one and only one part-time extra rest day per week;
15. Part-time extra rest days cannot be attributed on a day that is already a rest day, or a week-end day;

We add a constraint in order to balance the output schedules : there should not be more than 3 consecutive
days of rest. This constraint can prevent agents from having long vacations once and a sparse schedule for
the rest of the frame.

In order to simplify the problem, we can observe that it is possible to consider weeks of 6 days. Indeed, if
a week-end is worked, it is worked entirely. Plus, because of Constraint 4, an agent working on Saturday
evening can only work on Sunday evening (no day shift are required on week-end by virtue of Constraint 2).
It is also necessary to have more agents working in the morning than in the evening. Consequently, we will
assume that an agent working in the morning on Saturday, will have to work on Sunday morning as well.
On the other hand, we can also notice that Constraints 6 and 7 are redundant. As a matter of fact, if an
agent has one rest day in 5 days, then they will not work more than 48h during the week and will not work
5 consecutive days either.
Taking into consideration that the number of agents is odd, in combination with constraints 8 and 12,
we can show that the week-end total staff number cannot be constant, so that it will be impossible to satisfy
the constraint 13 strictly. In the general case, let’s assume that our odd number of agents is 2N + 1, with N
being a positive integer number. Then, if agent 1 works on the first week-end, there will be N + 1 working
agents on odd week-ends and N on even week-ends, and the opposite if agent 1 rests on week-end 1. An
illustration is provided in figure 1.
Nonetheless, Constraint 13 is not a constraint per se as it is a "best-effort" measure. It will be managed
using the goal function of the optimization problem.
One last observation that can be made is that for the sake of equity regarding the number of rested
week-ends, the number of weeks must be even. This is once again a consequence of constraints 8 and 12.

As an extension to this work, we applied the same model to another service in the same hospital: the
caregivers. This team is subject to slightly different constraints:
• The team is composed of 9 evenly skilled agents, including one with a part-timer.
• The service only needs 3 agents in the morning and 2 in the evening, every day.
• It is expected that the staff cannot fill the service needs. Consequently, we consider an additional
agent on week-ends to fill this gap. In practise, managers would ask for such an agent from the
hospital rotation workforce.
